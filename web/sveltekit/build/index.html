What a briefing contains

Every claim cites a public record from FEMA, NOAA, USGS, or city open data. Riprap reads 31 data sources; the methodology was last updated 2026-09-26.

Each briefing routes through a fixed taxonomy of public-record specialists. Each Stone is a class of evidence. Together they form the briefing, and every claim in the output traces back to the Stone that produced it.

Cornerstone, the hazard reader
What the ground remembers.
Keystone, the asset register
What's exposed.
Touchstone, the live observer
What's happening now.
Lodestone, the projector
What's coming.
Capstone, the synthesizer
Writes it all down with citations.

Riprap returns evidence, not advice.

Riprap composes public-record data into a written briefing. It does not predict damage to specific properties or substitute for professional engineering judgment. For personal property decisions, contact your local resilience office or hazard-mitigation program.

Independent open-source project. Not affiliated with FEMA, NOAA, USGS, or any city government. Data is used under each source's open-data terms.

This is open beta. Methodology and source attribution are stable. Per-city coverage is expanding. Send feedback.

Built to USWDS, WCAG 2.2 AA, Section 508 and the Plain Writing Act. Riprap is open source under Apache-2.0. All foundation models are Apache-2.0. All data comes from public-record federal, state and city sources. No commercial APIs are contacted at runtime. Riprap v0.7.0.
