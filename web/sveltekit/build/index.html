Riprap, open-source flood evidence for New York City

The flood record for any New York City block, cited line by line.

Riprap joins street sensors, 311 complaints, flood maps and storm records for an address or community district into one page. Every sentence carries its source and date, and the answer is the record's own words: no language model writes it.

Runs in seconds on a laptop: no GPU, no API keys.

Open models, open problems

I fine-tuned three open models for New York: one forecasts surge at the Battery, one reads satellite scenes after storms, one maps paved, green and tree-covered land. Each is labelled experimental, states its tested accuracy in every sentence, and has a measured baseline to beat.

  • Storm surge at the Battery

    Experimental

    Forecasts how far the water may run above the predicted tide over the next four days. A Granite TTM r2 fine-tune.

    Measured
    Mean error of 11.5 cm against 13.3 cm for a simple baseline, over 635 four-day windows since January 2025.
    The open problem
    It foresaw 1 of the 23 windows that reached minor flood stage. Catching those is the next result to earn.

  • Can satellites see street flooding?

    Experimental

    Maps new surface water in Sentinel-2 scenes after Ida and other heavy rains. A Prithvi-EO 2.0 fine-tune.

    Measured
    Not yet: new water within 500 m of 17 of 153 surveyed Ida marks (11%), where chance gives 14%. Help find the signal.
    The open problem
    Street and basement flooding is hard to see from orbit. Beating chance at the surveyed marks is the bar.

    • See it answer

  • Paved, green and tree canopy

    Experimental

    Estimates how much of each 10 m pixel is tree canopy, grass, paving or roof, in 2018, 2021, 2024 and 2026. A TerraMind fine-tune, trained on the city's own 2017 six-inch map.

    Measured
    On squares it never trained on, it reads a typical district's paved share 1.7 points above the city's own 2021 map. The adapter it replaced read 16 to 18 points high.
    The open problem
    Maps of different summers differ by more than two images of one summer, so it cannot yet show change between years. Telling real change from the images is the next result to earn.

Built on 23 public sources

City, state and federal records, read live or from their published files and sorted into five Stones. No commercial data and no scores. Each record has its own portal; we know of no other tool that reads them together for one address.

Hazard maps and storm records Cornerstone
FEMA's effective and preliminary flood maps, NYC DEP stormwater scenarios for today, 2050 and 2080, the 2012 Sandy inundation extent, USGS Hurricane Ida high-water marks, USGS terrain
Places at risk Keystone
NYC public schools, MTA subway entrances, NYCHA developments, hospitals, active DOB construction permits, NYC Planning's floodplain counts
Live sensors and complaints Touchstone
FloodNet street sensors, NYC 311 flood complaints, NOAA tide gauges, USGS stream gauges, National Weather Service observations
Forecasts and projections Lodestone
National Weather Service alerts and water-level forecasts, NPCC4 sea-level projections
How answers are written Capstone
Turns each record into one cited sentence and sets the answer's lead (Yes, No, a count) by fixed rules, not a language model.

FloodNet data is licensed CC-BY-NC-SA 4.0 and the NPCC4 projections CC-BY-NC 4.0; the other sources are public domain or open. See every source and licence.

How a briefing is made

For developers and AI agents

Every briefing is also data. Over HTTP you get the full result as JSON with its trace. Over MCP, seven tools return each finding with its figures, its source URL and the date of its data, plus the list of sources that did not answer. No language model is needed.

Riprap reports evidence, not advice. For flooding right now, FloodNet sensor dashboard, National Weather Service, Notify NYC; an official flood zone, FEMA Flood Map Service Center; insurance and what to do at home, FloodHelpNY; people and housing in the floodplain, NYC Planning Community District Profiles; maps to explore, Rainproof NYC Flood Map. Independent and open source. Not affiliated with FEMA, NOAA, USGS or the City of New York.

Build it with us

Riprap is built by Adam Munawar Rahman, open source under Apache-2.0. I'd like help with three things: a pilot in a studio course, council office or engineering firm; a second city; and better ground truth for the surge and satellite models.

  • Run a pilot

  • Bring your data

    Each model has a backtest and a baseline. When a test reruns, the briefing's accuracy sentence updates from its results.
