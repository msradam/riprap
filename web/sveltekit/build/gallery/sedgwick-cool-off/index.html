Answer

From the sources consulted: NYC Parks lists spray showers at 9 parks or playgrounds within 800 m of this address (lists updated 2026-10-03 and 2026-09-15, copied 2026-10-05); the six nearest: Sedgwick Playground (spray shower, 172 m), Plimpton Playground (spray shower, 370 m), Half-Nelson Playground (spray shower, 437 m), Quisqueya Plgd (spray shower, 547 m), Ogden Plimpton Playground (spray shower, 565 m), Wallenberg Plgd (spray shower, 672 m).1 Spray showers and outdoor pools run in summer only, and the list does not say whether one is working today. Cooling centers are separate: the city opens them only during a heat emergency and lists them then at finder.nyc.gov/coolingcenters (or call 311).1

Terms on this page

Measured, Modeled, Proxy:
how directly a source observes heat. Measured sources record it; modeled sources estimate or forecast it; proxy sources indicate it indirectly.

This is an automated hazard-exposure briefing produced by Riprap from live and precomputed data sources. It is informational only and not a substitute for a professional risk assessment. These are public records about a place, not an assessment of any property or of the people who live there. Riprap computes no score or rating, and records can be missing where people report less.

During a heat emergency the city lists its cooling centers at finder.nyc.gov/coolingcenters; heat safety guidance is at nyc.gov/health.

The NYC Health Department's Heat Vulnerability Index (2023, from 2016 to 2020 data) scores University Heights (South)-Morris Heights, the neighbourhood around this address, at 5 out of 5, the highest risk group. The index ranks neighbourhoods against each other: the department says it "uses a statistical model to summarize the most important factors of neighborhood heat risk: surface temperature, green space, home air conditioning, and income". It is not a measurement of heat at an address, and the department says "All neighborhoods have residents at risk for heat illness and death". For University Heights (South)-Morris Heights the department's file gives 79.7% of households with air conditioning (a survey estimate it shares across neighbouring neighbourhoods) and 25.9% green space. The index scores University Heights (South)-Morris Heights as one area: no one of the neighbourhoods in its name has a score of its own. The area is the 2020 Neighborhood Tabulation Area the index is published for, which can carry another name than the neighbourhood in the address above. Of who heat harms most, the department writes that Black New Yorkers suffer disproportionate health impacts from heat "due to social and economic disparities", and that "These disparities stem from structural racism, which includes neighborhood disinvestment, racist housing policies, fewer job opportunities and lower pay, and less access to high-quality education and health care" (NYC Health Department, Interactive Heat Vulnerability Index). This address is in or beside the public housing development Sedgwick (NYCHA's map of its developments): the neighbourhood's figures, its air conditioning share especially, are not the development's. Out of scope

This briefing does not measure the temperature inside a building, give health advice, or predict the heat on a given day at a given address. Air temperature and an outdoor heat exposure index are mapped on the NYC Urban Heat Portal (https://urbanheat.nyc), by BetaNYC. Where a source was offline at run time, the relevant section omits that signal.
