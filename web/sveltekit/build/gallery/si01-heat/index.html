In brief

The surface of this area ran 1.1°F warmer than the city's land average over 18 clear summer Landsat images (surface temperature, not air temperature).1 27.8% of this area is under tree canopy and 51.4% is paved or built over on the city's 2017 land cover map.2 This area scores 2 out of 5 on the Health Department's Heat Vulnerability Index, a rank among neighbourhoods and not a measurement.3

Experimental: a satellite land-cover model estimates that 53.9% of this area is paved or built over and 43.6% is green (17.4% tree canopy), on Sentinel-2 scenes from 2026 (1.8% water); no change between years is read from it. Limits: it estimates the share of each 10 m satellite pixel that is canopy, grass, paving, roof, water or bare ground, learned from the city's 2017 six-inch map, so detail finer than about 30 m is blurred and a tree over a street counts as canopy; against The Nature Conservancy's 2021 six-inch map of the city, on squares it never trained on, it read a typical district's paved share 1.7 points above the map's (at most 7.8 points off in the 46 districts with enough test ground) and its mean error per land-cover group was 4.8 points at best, more than the 3.0 points of the city's 2017 map read as if it were 2021, so the 2017 map is the more accurate source for its year; citywide its maps show 4.2 to 5.0 points less tree canopy than the city's 2017 map in 3 of its 4 yearly maps (2018, 2021, 2026) and 3.2 points more in 1 of its 4 yearly maps (2024), which is the model and its images and not the trees, so its canopy share can be several points off; two images of one summer differ by under 2.0 points in a district's paved share 19 times in 20, but between 2018 and 2024 the shares of 49 of 59 districts differ by more, so its maps of different years are not compared. Rely on the city's 2017 land cover map and The Nature Conservancy's 2021 map (both 6 inch) and the city's building footprints for a survey. Terms on this page

Measured, Modeled, Proxy:
how directly a source observes heat. Measured sources record it; modeled sources estimate or forecast it; proxy sources indicate it indirectly.

This is an automated hazard-exposure briefing produced by Riprap from live and precomputed data sources. It is informational only and not a substitute for a professional risk assessment. These are public records about a place, not an assessment of any property or of the people who live there. Riprap computes no score or rating, and records can be missing where people report less.

During a heat emergency the city lists its cooling centers at finder.nyc.gov/coolingcenters; heat safety guidance is at nyc.gov/health.

Out of scope

This briefing does not measure the temperature inside a building, give health advice, or predict the heat on a given day at a given address. Air temperature and an outdoor heat exposure index are mapped on the NYC Urban Heat Portal (https://urbanheat.nyc), by BetaNYC. Where a source was offline at run time, the relevant section omits that signal.
