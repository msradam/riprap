Answer

Yes.

2 FloodNet community sensors within 600 m have logged 14 above-curb flood events in the last 3 years.1

USGS surveyed 2 Hurricane Ida high-water marks within 800 m of this address; the highest stood 0.76 ft above ground; the highest water surface elevation was 48.2 ft NAVD88.2 Nearest mark: Intersection of 182nd St. and 90th Ave., Jamaica, Queens (174 m away).2

This address sits in FEMA flood zone X (an area of minimal flood hazard), per NFHL FIRM panel 3604970234F, effective 2007.3

This address is outside the modeled flooding in the NYC DEP stormwater scenario (2.13 in/hr, 2050 SLR).4

Elevation 14.87 m; this point is higher than 29% of the ground within 200 m (the 29th percentile; a low percentile means a local low spot where water collects); HAND (height above the nearest drainage channel) 0.0 m; TWI (topographic wetness index; higher means water tends to collect) 11.78.5 Local basin relief (the rise from this point to the highest ground within 750 m): 35.02 m.5

Peak depth recorded by the sensors in good working order: 815 mm on 2026-05-20.1 1 of the sensors that logged events is flagged by FloodNet for maintenance, so its depths are not used for the peak.

Terms on this page

NAVD88:
an elevation above a fixed survey reference (the North American Vertical Datum of 1988), not a water depth.
SLR:
sea-level rise.
above-curb flood event:
a flood event logged by a FloodNet street sensor, which measures the depth of water on the street; the count uses FloodNet's own event records.
Measured, Modeled, Proxy, Synthetic:
how directly a source observes flooding. Measured sources record it; modeled sources simulate a scenario; proxy sources, such as 311 complaints, indicate it indirectly; synthetic layers are generated, not observed.
