Answer

From an experimental model, not a measurement:

Experimental A satellite model on Sentinel-2 scenes from 2021-09-02, the day after Hurricane Ida, compared with 2021-08-13, showed 1,100 m² of new surface water in this area (98% of it observed); of 10 other heavy-rain events since 2017 with a clear pass over this place, it showed new water after 6 (2017-10-29, 2023-09-11, 2023-11-22, 2024-03-23, 2025-03-05, 2026-08-03).1 Limits: a satellite passes every few days and sees 10 to 20 m pixels, so street and basement flooding that drains within hours is invisible to it, and its training labels were the base model's own output for Hurricane Ida, not surveyed flooding; of 153 high-water marks USGS surveyed after Ida, it showed new water within 500 m of 17 (11%), no better than chance (14% of the land it saw lies that close to its new water).1 Rely on the surveyed and measured record (USGS high-water marks, FloodNet sensors) and NYC DEP's stormwater flood maps.1

Terms on this page

SLR:
sea-level rise.
MLLW:
mean lower low water, the average of each day's lower low tide; NOAA tide readings are measured against it.
above-curb flood event:
a flood event logged by a FloodNet street sensor, which measures the depth of water on the street; the count uses FloodNet's own event records.
Measured, Modeled, Proxy:
how directly a source observes flooding. Measured sources record it; modeled sources simulate a scenario; proxy sources, such as 311 complaints, indicate it indirectly.

This is an automated hazard-exposure briefing produced by Riprap from live and precomputed data sources. It is informational only and not a substitute for a professional risk assessment.
